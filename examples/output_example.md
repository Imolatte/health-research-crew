# Magnesium and Sleep Quality: An Evidence-Based Review

## Executive Summary
Research indicates that magnesium supplementation may modestly improve sleep quality, particularly in older adults and individuals with magnesium deficiency. Evidence from randomized controlled trials is promising but limited by small sample sizes. Confidence level: Moderate.

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: Magnesium improves subjective sleep quality in older adults
**Evidence Level:** Moderate
**What studies show:** A double-blind RCT (n=46, age 60+) found that 500mg magnesium daily for 8 weeks significantly improved sleep quality scores (PSQI), sleep efficiency, and early morning awakening compared to placebo.
**Quality of evidence:** Randomized controlled trial; limited by small sample size and single population.

### Finding 2: Magnesium regulates melatonin and GABA pathways
**Evidence Level:** Moderate (mechanistic)
**What studies show:** Magnesium acts as a natural NMDA antagonist and GABA agonist, both pathways involved in sleep regulation. It also supports melatonin synthesis. This mechanistic basis supports the observed clinical effects.
**Quality of evidence:** Animal studies + mechanistic human data; direct causation in humans not fully established.

### Finding 3: Benefits may be limited to deficient individuals
**Evidence Level:** Weak to Moderate
**What studies show:** A 2021 systematic review found inconsistent results across populations, with strongest effects in those with low magnesium levels. Benefits in healthy adults with adequate magnesium are unclear.
**Quality of evidence:** Systematic review; heterogeneous studies, no definitive meta-analysis available.

## Practical Recommendations

1. **Consider magnesium if you have poor sleep and suspect deficiency** - particularly if you eat few leafy greens, nuts, or whole grains. *Evidence basis: RCT showing benefits in deficient older adults.*

2. **Typical studied dose: 300-500mg elemental magnesium daily** - magnesium glycinate or citrate forms are better absorbed. *Evidence basis: Forms used in clinical trials.*

3. **Don't expect dramatic effects if you're not deficient** - overall sleep improvement is modest (1-2 points on PSQI scale). *Evidence basis: Systematic review findings.*

4. **Consider dietary sources first** - pumpkin seeds, almonds, spinach, black beans are rich sources. *Evidence basis: Dietary adequacy is sufficient for most adults.*

## Important Caveats
- Most studies are small (< 100 participants) and short-term (< 3 months)
- Research is strongest in older adults; less data for younger populations
- High doses (> 350mg supplements) may cause diarrhea or interact with medications
- Consult a healthcare professional before supplementing, especially with kidney disease or if taking medications
